Privacy policy — OpenClaw Neuro

Last updated: 5 October 2026.

OpenClaw Neuro is a private integration operated by Anatoliy Martynov for the owner's Google account.

Data accessed and purpose

With the owner's consent, the integration accesses Google Drive file metadata and selected file contents, Google Sheets ranges and Google Docs text to carry out the owner's requests. It can create and modify approved resources. Its Google OAuth scope is https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive, which grants broad Drive access; application restrictions are narrower than that grant.

Processing and sharing

Requested file contents and results can be included in the OpenClaw assistant context and sent to the configured AI service provider to fulfil the request. Answers may be returned through Telegram. Google, the configured AI provider and Telegram process the data they receive under their respective policies. Do not submit data that you are not authorised to process through these services.

The operator does not sell Google user data, use it for advertising, or use it to train general-purpose AI models. The integration's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Storage and protection

OAuth client credentials and refresh credentials are stored locally in Windows Credential Manager under the owner's Windows profile, in the separate OpenClaw/Neuro/Google namespace. Integration audit records contain operation status and request hashes rather than raw OAuth credentials. Local resource metadata, assistant conversation history and task results may persist on the owner's computer; requested answers may also persist in Telegram and in provider systems under their policies.

Retention and revocation

Local resource records and task history are retained until the owner removes them under the relevant application's controls. This integration has no automatic retention deadline. The owner can revoke Google access in Google Account permissions, remove local application credentials and records, and delete files from Drive. Revocation stops future authorised access but does not automatically erase prior conversation history or task outputs. Credentials must be stored again if access is subsequently authorised.
